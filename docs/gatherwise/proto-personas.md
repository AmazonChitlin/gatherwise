# Gatherwise Proto-Personas

Date: 2026-07-13
Status: living assumptions, not validated personas

## 1. First-Time Small-Event Organizer

- Situation: Planning a small public-facing event, pop-up, or community gathering without much jurisdiction-specific experience.
- Functional goal: Figure out what to check next without already knowing permit names, agency boundaries, or municipal terminology.
- Emotional goal: Feel less overwhelmed and less afraid of missing something important.
- Biggest uncertainty: Which event details actually matter to the rules and which agency is relevant.
- Existing workaround: Search city websites, ask friends, search social media groups, or guess based on similar events.
- Information they trust: Official agency links, plain-language explanations tied to sources, and concrete reasons why a requirement appeared.
- Likely failure point: Quitting when intake questions feel too detailed or when results feel too official without being understandable.
- Accessibility considerations: Plain language, predictable step structure, mobile readability, strong contrast, keyboard access, and clear recovery from missing information.
- What would make Gatherwise feel useful within 90 seconds: A clear explanation of what it does, a fast path into intake, and a source-backed summary that says what to confirm first in everyday language.

## 2. Experienced Organizer Expanding Into A New Jurisdiction

- Situation: Has run events before, but not in this specific city, county, or rule area.
- Functional goal: Translate existing event knowledge into the new jurisdiction quickly and identify what is different.
- Emotional goal: Feel efficient rather than reset to beginner mode.
- Biggest uncertainty: Which local rules, contacts, and lead times change when the jurisdiction changes.
- Existing workaround: Reuse old checklists, compare city websites manually, email agencies, or ask venue partners.
- Information they trust: Jurisdiction-specific evidence, change comparison, source recency, and explicit notes on what is unknown.
- Likely failure point: Results feel generic, duplicate what they already know, or fail to explain jurisdiction-specific differences.
- Accessibility considerations: Dense but scannable summaries, comparison clarity, optional detail depth, and support for low-friction keyboard navigation.
- What would make Gatherwise feel useful within 90 seconds: Immediate confirmation of the supported jurisdiction, a sharp view of what changed, and evidence anchors tied to the new locality.

## 3. Vendor Or Food Operator Joining Someone Else’s Event

- Situation: Participating in an event organized by another party and needs to understand what responsibilities still fall on the vendor or operator.
- Functional goal: Separate organizer responsibilities from vendor/operator responsibilities and know what documents or approvals to confirm before showing up.
- Emotional goal: Feel protected from showing up unprepared or non-compliant because someone else handled the event.
- Biggest uncertainty: Which requirements attach to the event overall versus their booth, food operation, or retail activity.
- Existing workaround: Ask the organizer, copy prior event prep, rely on marketplace instructions, or search county/state sites directly.
- Information they trust: Official sources, explicit role boundaries, and lists that show whether an item depends on organizer facts or vendor facts.
- Likely failure point: Results blur shared responsibility and personal responsibility, or they require details the vendor does not control.
- Accessibility considerations: Clear ownership labels, simple explanations, readable mobile layouts during on-the-go prep, and tolerance for partial information.
- What would make Gatherwise feel useful within 90 seconds: A visible path for participants, plain wording about what they personally need to confirm, and obvious source links for food, tax, or vendor-specific items.

## 4. Recruiter Evaluating The Handshake Project

- Situation: Reviewing the project as evidence of product thinking, technical depth, AI judgment, and execution quality.
- Functional goal: Understand what the product does, what Paul built, where AI helps, and where deterministic logic or evidence boundaries take over.
- Emotional goal: Feel confident that the project demonstrates intentional architecture and responsible AI use rather than demo-only gloss.
- Biggest uncertainty: Whether the project is a serious hybrid system or a surface-level AI wrapper around static content.
- Existing workaround: Skim the repo, scan screenshots, infer architecture from code structure, and look for clear articulation of ownership and tradeoffs.
- Information they trust: Architecture diagrams, explicit AI boundaries, evidence handling rules, repo docs, test coverage, and honest statements about unsupported areas.
- Likely failure point: They cannot tell what is manual versus AI-driven, cannot identify Paul’s contribution, or cannot map the product flow quickly.
- Accessibility considerations: Fast information scent, concise architecture framing, scannable visuals, and plain-language explanation of system boundaries.
- What would make Gatherwise feel useful within 90 seconds: A short story of the problem, a visible architecture overview, a clear AI boundary, and an explanation of why the manual path still matters.

## Cross-Persona Notes

- All four proto-personas are currently assumptions that need validation through interviews, prototypes, or observed task-based testing.
- Trust, explanation, and speed-to-usefulness appear more central than feature breadth.
- Accessibility needs should be treated as baseline product quality, not a special-case add-on.
